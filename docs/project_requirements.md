# Final Project Requirements — ESE 5420 (Fall 2026)

Source: course syllabus (ESE 5420, Statistics for Data Science, Prof. Victor M. Preciado), with notes from the project field guide in `project_ideas/`.

## Overview

- Weight: **20% of the course grade** (midterms 60%, homework 20%).
- Groups of **up to 3 students**.
- Developed in four stages so the work gets feedback before it is graded.
- No in-class presentations; both final deliverables are evaluated outside class time.
- The last two weeks of term (Tue 24 Nov, Tue 1 Dec, Thu 3 Dec) are reserved for project work; no new material is introduced.

## Timeline

| Stage | Due | Graded |

- The proposal and early draft carry no marks, but **both are required** and both receive written feedback. Their purpose is to catch a project that won't work while there is still time to change it.
- **No late window**: unlike homework, the final project closes at its deadline. Accommodations must be arranged with the instructor in advance as individual Canvas deadlines.

## Final deliverables

1. **Paper**
   - Up to **6 pages** in **NeurIPS format**.
   - References and appendices do not count toward the page limit.
   - Must include a short **AI use statement** in the acknowledgements, naming the assistants used and what they were used for. It does not count against the page limit.
2. **Video**
   - **5 minutes**, summarizing the paper.

## Scope and expectations

- **Novelty is not required.** The goal is to emulate the *format* of a machine learning paper, not the research contribution behind one.
- Full-credit recipe: *a careful comparison of methods from the course, on a public dataset that is already usable, honestly reported.*
- Methods should come from the course modules: probability review, learning theory (kNN, bias–variance), optimization (GD, SGD, Adam), linear regression, classification (logistic regression, ROC), resampling (CV, bootstrap), model selection and regularization (subset selection, ridge, lasso, PCR), trees and ensembles (bagging, random forests, boosting, XGBoost), neural networks (MLP, RNN, autoencoders), and unsupervised learning (PCA, K-means, hierarchical clustering).
- Field-guide notes: neural networks are optional — e.g. lasso vs. random forest vs. boosting vs. MLP is a complete project. Include a simple baseline, evaluate on held-out data honestly (no leakage; time-respecting splits for time series), and report negative results as legitimate findings.

## AI use policy

- AI assistants may be used at every stage: choosing a topic, writing code, drafting the paper, and preparing the video.
- The work, and responsibility for its correctness, remains the group's.
- Disclose AI use in the paper's acknowledgements (see above).
